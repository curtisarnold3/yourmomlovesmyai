E

Scenario: Taiwan goes dark

What happens to this network if Taiwan's chip output stops? hypothetical · not a forecast

Pick a disruption and step through time. Physical links turn amber as inventories drain and red when they're cut. Each company is shaded by a modeled stock drawdown built from three transparent ingredients: how much it depends on Taiwan's output, how much it sells into Taiwan's fabs, and its historical sensitivity to the chip sector. You set the two biggest unknowns with the sliders.

How this model works, and every assumption in it

For each company: drawdown = worst-case × market-fear curve(time) × (0.25 × sector beta + 0.75 × exposure hit), where the exposure hit is the larger of (needs Taiwan output × share of Taiwan output lost) and (sells into Taiwan × share of that market lost). Taiwan-based companies take an extra asset hit that grows over time and is largest in the invasion case. One round of spill-over then pulls each company slightly toward its supply-chain neighbors. Samsung is treated as a possible alternative foundry, so it can come out ahead. Exposure percentages marked "assumed" are judgment calls, not reported figures. Change them and the picture changes.

Factual anchors: TSMC makes ~90% of the most advanced chips (MarketScreener); ~85–90% of TSMC's leading-edge output remains in Taiwan (SemiconductorX); TSMC makes every Nvidia AI GPU, and its Arizona fab was not producing CoWoS packaging (PolicyRisk); TSMC takes ~70–80% of annual EUV output (PolicyRisk); Micron makes more than 65% of its DRAM in Taiwan, with its largest HBM site in Taichung (Taipei Times, 3DTested). Everything else is a modeling assumption. This is an educational stress test of supply-chain dependence. It does not predict any event, its likelihood, or real price moves, and it is not investment advice.
