    Situational Awareness

    The AI Trade: How a Powerful Thesis Meets Portfolio Risk

    In June 2024 a 22-year-old former OpenAI researcher published a 165-page essay arguing that AGI might arrive this decade. Within two years his hedge fund reportedly grew from about $225 million to $45 billion. Then, in one month, it was forced to sell its entire public stock book.

    This is the story of how a thesis can be largely right and the trade can still break.

    CHAPTER 02

    The Rise of the Fund

    Question: How did a small initial investment become a $45 billion fund?

    The fund deployed its first dollars on November 1, 2024, with roughly $225 million from Stripe's Collison brothers, Nat Friedman, Daniel Gross and others. Twenty months later, reported assets were 200 times larger. But "assets" isn't the same as "profit."

    Where could $45 billion come from?

    AUM ≠ P&L. Assets under management move for at least six reasons: investment gains or losses, new subscriptions, redemptions, asset transfers, deleveraging, and changes in how "assets" are defined. A drop from $45B to $10B does not prove investors lost $35B.

    CHAPTER 07

    The Logic-Short / Memory-Long Trade

    Question: How does a relative-value view become a position?

    This is the source's framing, simplified. In the filings, the fund's largest bearish chip line was a put on SMH, an ETF that holds memory makers too, so the real book was never a clean pair. A relative-value trade doesn't bet that memory goes up. It bets that memory does better than logic. Put that way, a market crash that hits both sides equally costs nothing. But that only holds if both baskets move together, and in July 2026 they didn't.

    CHAPTER 08

    What Four-Times Leverage Means

    Question: How does leverage magnify gains and losses?

    CNBC's David Faber said the fund was levered "as much as four times." That phrase can describe very different balance sheets. The lab below starts with one: $100 million of investor equity carrying $400 million of positions, half long and half short.

    Hypothetical balance sheet · exact scale
    Interactive · Leverage laboratory
    HYPOTHETICAL
    Gross = |Long| + |Short|  ·  Net = Long − Short
    Gross leverage = Gross ÷ Equity  ·  Net leverage = Net ÷ Equity
    Funding convention here: longs are paid for with equity first, then borrowing. Short-sale proceeds stay at the broker as collateral. Idle cash and short proceeds earn no interest in this model.
    4× gross does not mean "borrowed three times equity." In the balanced example, $200M of longs on $100M of equity needs only $100M of borrowing. The other $200M is shorts, which raise cash that stays locked at the broker as collateral. A long-only 4× book would need $300M of borrowing. Swaps and options can build the same exposure with no "loan" at all, and each structure carries different margin terms.

    Question: What happens when everything moves against a leveraged portfolio at once?

    Interactive · Market-stress & margin simulator
    HYPOTHETICAL · ILLUSTRATIVE MARGIN TERMS

    About the margin setting: FINRA Rule 4210's standard maintenance minimums are 25% of long value and 30% of short value. Under standard Reg T rules a 4× gross book isn't reachable at all. Hedge funds use risk-based portfolio margin, which can be lower for hedged books and higher for concentrated ones. The 15% default is an illustrative stand-in, not a known term of this fund. The private Anthropic stake is treated as non-marginable (100% equity-funded) and is never force-sold.

    A falling balance alone doesn't trigger a margin call. Whether and when a broker demands more collateral depends on the financing agreement, the collateral posted, position volatility and concentration, and the counterparty's discretion. Bloomberg reported the fund sold positions to meet margin calls. The actual terms are not public.

    In July the longs fell hard, the software shorts rose, and the chip shorts barely moved. A hedge that moves against you turns into a second loss.

    The whole picture, one layer at a time

    A compelling thesis doesn't guarantee a successful trade. Position sizing, leverage, market timing, correlations and liquidity decide whether an investor survives long enough for the thesis to play out.

    What we don't know yet: the fund's actual position sizes in July, its margin terms, the price Citadel paid, how much of the $45B→$10B change came from losses versus redemptions or the sale discount, and whether the thesis will ultimately prove right. Several of its core names were still far above their 2025 levels even after July.
