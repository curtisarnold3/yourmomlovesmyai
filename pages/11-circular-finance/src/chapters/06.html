Promises versus cash

Promises vs. cash

Say you sign a lease for a very nice apartment. Rent is $4,000 a month, five years, and you've never earned more than $2,000 a month. Is that a lie? No. You might be about to get a big promotion. You might also be about to get a roommate. The lease itself doesn't tell us which.

That's what a lot of the AI numbers look like right now. A backlog or RPO (remaining performance obligations) is a signed promise to buy something later. It's a real contract. It is not cash in a bank account. So let's put the promises on one side, the cash on the other, and see how far apart they are. We'll draw promises as dashed bars, and we'll never add them to cash.

      Take-or-pay versus flexible

      Take-or-pay means you pay whether or not you use the capacity, like a gym membership you can't cancel. Flexible means you can scale back, like a month-to-month rental. A take-or-pay backlog is firmer for the seller. A flexible one is firmer for the buyer. When you read a big backlog number, the first question is which kind it is. Most of the headlines don't say.
