Where does the debt actually sit?

Off the balance sheet

Think about a restaurant owner who doesn't own the building. A landlord does. The owner pays rent, the landlord holds the mortgage, and nobody calls that a scheme. It's just how restaurants work.

Now make the restaurant a hyperscaler (a giant cloud company like Microsoft, Amazon, Google or Meta), the building a data center financed by a multibillion-dollar bond, and the landlord a joint venture set up for the purpose. Same idea, bigger zeros. Seven structures keep showing up in the AI buildout, and each one moves debt or risk from one place to another. Each card below has a diagram. Press Next to step through it.

First, what the phrase means

"Off balance sheet" usually means the debt sits inside a separate legal entity that the sponsoring company doesn't fully count as its own. That's often legal, often disclosed in the footnotes, and often for sensible reasons. The reader's job is to find the footnote and add the pieces back up. We aren't alleging anything improper in any deal here.
