One measure worth tracking

After ten chapters of arrows, here is one question worth keeping in mind: how much of the cash moving around the loop is paid by end customers, the people and businesses actually using AI? It is not the only question, and the number below comes from a toy model whose main default is an assumption, not a measurement.

One number worth tracking: the share of the loop's cash that is paid by end customers. Cash from lenders and outside investors is also outside money, but it is not end-user demand. It has to be repaid or earn a return, and that return has to come from end customers or from someone else's future money. Cash that started with a loop member and came back to it, like a chipmaker's investment in a customer that buys the chipmaker's systems, is recycled. Promises, such as contracts and backstops, aren't cash and stay out of every pile.

A low end-customer share doesn't mean anything is wrong. It tells you how much of the loop still depends on future customers showing up. We can't read the true value off any filing, so we give a range and let you explore what would move it.
